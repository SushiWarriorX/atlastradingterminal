## Experiment 2, pre-registered 2026-11-11 04:20 UTC (written BEFORE the run): cross-sectional crypto momentum

- **Rule (fixed):** every 7th day of the panel the portfolio is rebalanced at the close and traded at the next open. ## Experiment 4, pre-registered 2026-11-14 10:40 UTC (written BEFORE the run): BTC funding carry with a funding-regime switch
A different kind of idea from experiments 1 to 3 (market-neutral: it does not bet on BTC's direction), chosen because the 2026-11-10 finding showed Hyperliquid funding was far above Treasury bills in 2024 but near them in 2026, so a switch is the only version that could make sense. Not a rescue variant of anything.
- **Position when ON:** half of the capital is long BTC spot, the other half is cash margin for an equal-notional short BTC perpetual on Hyperliquid. The two price legs are assumed to cancel exactly (see biases). The strategy earns the perpetual's funding on the short notional (the short is paid when funding is positive and pays when it is negative). **The margin cash earns NOTHING while ON (conservative: USDC margin does not earn a bill rate).** So per day, ON return on total capital = 0.5 x (sum of that UTC day's hourly funding rates).
- **Position when OFF:** all capital in 3-month Treasury bills at the FRED DTB3 rate (percent per year, carried forward over missing days), daily return = DTB3 / 100 / 365.
- **Switch rule (fixed):** at the end of each UTC day t compute F14 = (sum of all hourly funding rates in days t-13 to t) / 14 x 365 (annualised funding on notional, a fraction). Be ON on day t+1 if 0.5 x F14 >= DTB3(t)/100 + 0.01 (the carry would beat bills by at least 1 percentage point a year before switching costs), otherwise OFF. Start OFF; the first decision is made once 14 days of funding exist.
- **Costs:** every switch (OFF to ON or ON to OFF) costs 0.30% of total capital (conservative against about 0.18% estimated from 10 bps spot fee, 5 bps perp fee and 10 bps slippage on half the capital per leg). No other costs.
- **Data (fixed):** Hyperliquid BTC `fundingHistory` records (hourly), used only from the first record that has a 1-hour spacing to the previous one (the earlier records in May and early June 2023 are spaced 8 hours apart and their unit is not verified, so they are excluded). Last day = the last complete UTC day before the run. FRED DTB3 daily. BTC daily closes from the Coinbase cache for the buy-and-hold comparison only. One shared UTC daily calendar.
- **Split:** the first 70% of the days (after the 14-day warm-up) are for design (nothing is tuned there) and the last 30% are judged (sealed). One run. No variants: 14 days, 50/50 split, 1 percentage point, 0.30% are not to be changed after the result.
- **Comparisons over the sealed period (same costs and calendar):** always in bills; always-ON carry (one entry cost); BTC buy-and-hold.
- **Pass = ALL of, on the sealed period:** (a) annualised net return at least 1.0 percentage point above always-in-bills; (b) worst peak-to-trough drop of the equity curve no deeper than 3.0%; (c) annualised net return at least that of always-ON carry (otherwise the switch adds nothing). **Data check (not a pass condition):** no gap longer than 3 hours between funding records inside the sealed period; if there is one the run is logged INVALID, not failed.
- **If it fails** any condition it is logged "not yet" with the numbers and closed. If it passes it is only a candidate: it still needs the forward record, and the biases below stay attached.
- **Known biases / NOT modelled:** the price legs are assumed to cancel exactly (basis, hedge drift and the gap between the funding premium and the mark are ignored); exchange, counterparty and smart-contract risk on Hyperliquid; liquidation of the short if BTC rallies hard and margin is thin; USDC depeg; taxes; the funding rate Hyperliquid actually pays can differ slightly from the published record; Hyperliquid's history starts in 2023, one market cycle, so the sample is short and a pass would be weak evidence. DTB3 is a secondary-market rate, not the rate a retail account earns.

## Experiment 5, pre-registered 2026-11-14 13:40 UTC (written BEFORE any code or result): Bitcoin held only while US net liquidity is expanding
Budget: experiment 5 of at most 8 (see the budget guard above). Chosen because it uses a different information source from experiments 1 to 4 (the Federal Reserve's balance sheet, not Bitcoin's own price or funding) and has a popular story behind it, which is also a warning: popular stories are where data mining hides. The ETF idea is blocked on data.
- **Net liquidity (fixed definition):** for each Wednesday w, L(w) = Fed total assets (FRED WALCL, Wednesday level, millions of dollars) minus the Treasury General Account (FRED WDTGAL, Wednesday level, millions) minus overnight reverse repo (FRED RRPONTSYD on that Wednesday, billions; if that day has no value the latest earlier value within 3 days is used and counted), all converted to billions of dollars. Current FRED vintage (see biases).
- **Signal (fixed):** dL(w) = L(w) - L(w - 91 days). **ON if dL > 0** (net liquidity higher than 13 weeks earlier), otherwise OFF (exactly zero counts as OFF). No other length, level or smoothing is tried.
- **Decision and timing:** on every Friday's daily bar (UTC date), use the value for the Wednesday two days earlier (published Thursday after the close, so it was public before the Friday close); the weight (1.0 for ON, 0.0 for OFF) is decided at that Friday's close and applied from the next open, held until the next Friday's decision. If the Wednesday's value is missing, the previous state is kept and counted. Before the first signal exists the weight is 0. Same simulator, timing and costs as experiment 1 and the whole BTC lab (`btclab/weights.py`: 5 bps fee + 5 bps slippage on the amount traded, **cash earns nothing**).
- **Data:** Coinbase BTC-USD daily bars (the lab's own cache, 2015-07-20 to the last complete day) and the three FRED series above. **Data validity checks (not pass conditions; any failure logs the run INVALID, not failed):** the Wednesday series used is contiguous (no missing week), every L(w) between 3,000 and 9,000 billion dollars, and RRP carried forward on no more than 5% of Wednesdays.
- **Split:** the same sealed last 30% of the bars (index int(0.70 x bars) onward); the first 70% is design (nothing is tuned on it). Every comparator is simulated from the first sealed bar, starting flat, so entry costs are treated identically. One run. No variants.
- **Comparisons (sealed period, same simulator):** (i) BTC buy-and-hold; (ii) a constant-exposure hold at the strategy's average weight in the sealed period; (iii) the null: **1,000 random circular shifts** of the strategy's own sealed-period weight sequence by a whole number of weeks (between 8 weeks and the period length minus 8 weeks, seed 20261114). A shift keeps the exposure, the number of switches and the weekday of every switch exactly as in the strategy and only breaks the alignment with Bitcoin's returns, so beating the null means the TIMING mattered, not just holding less Bitcoin in a bad stretch.
- **Pass = ALL of, on the sealed period:** (a) Sharpe at least 0.10 above BTC buy-and-hold; (b) Sharpe above the 95th percentile of the 1,000 shifted schedules; (c) worst drop at least 10 percentage points shallower than BTC buy-and-hold's; (d) net return after costs above zero and at least 10 on/off switches in the sealed period.
- **If it fails** any condition it is logged "not yet" with the numbers and closed; no other lookbacks, definitions or filters are tried. If it passes it is only a candidate: still to be followed forward for 60 days before anything else.
- **Known biases / NOT modelled:** current-vintage FRED data (the Fed's weekly figures are rarely revised, but this is not a point-in-time vintage); "net liquidity" has several popular definitions and I fixed one before looking; the story is popular, so a pass could be crowded or luck; the sample holds only a handful of liquidity regimes (2017-18 shrinkage, 2020 expansion, 2022-23 tightening, the 2023 Treasury-account rebuild), so even a pass is weak evidence; cash earns nothing (this slightly hurts any rule that sits in cash); Bitcoin only, no leverage, no taxes; the Friday-close timing choice is mine.
