# ATLAS data-source loop (every 10 minutes)

Goal: keep adding **new, free, public data** to Signal, one verified source per cycle. Research only: no order controls, ever.

## Each cycle (do these in order; stop early and log if a step fails)
1. Read `atlas/DATA_SOURCES.md` (what is live, what is queued, what was rejected and why). Pick the first QUEUED candidate; if none, search the web for one new candidate (free, no key, official or clearly public, useful for BTC, US stocks or macro) and add it to the queue.
   Finding an ECB series (checked live 2026-11-17; the ECB website and the Data Portal pages are refused by the proxy): `GET https://data-api.ecb.europa.eu/service/dataflow/ECB?detail=allstubs` lists the ECB's own dataflows in one small request, `.../service/dataflow/all/<ID>?detail=allstubs` finds a flow of any agency (Eurostat's `LFSI` and `MNA` are served through it), and `.../service/data/<ID>/<key>?lastNObservations=3&format=csvdata` returns a few values with their title, unit, multiplier and status columns. Eurostat's own host (`ec.europa.eu`) is refused too: BLOCKED, do not go round it (loop-rules item 5). The `MNA` key has 14 dimensions (`FREQ.ADJUSTMENT.REF_AREA.COUNTERPART_AREA.REF_SECTOR.COUNTERPART_SECTOR.ACCOUNTING_ENTRY.STO.INSTR_ASSET.ACTIVITY.EXPENDITURE.UNIT_MEASURE.PRICES.TRANSFORMATION`) and `.../service/data/MNA/Q.Y.I10` followed by eleven dots lists every seasonally adjusted series of the euro area (280 series, 7 s); a key with too few dots answers HTTP 400. A dataflow too big to list in one request (the whole euro-area slice of `MIR` gave no answer in 120 s) is listed by narrowing the key one slice at a time (counterpart sector and data type: 44 to 101 series in 5 to 13 s). The `MIR` slice of rates on OUTSTANDING amounts (`IR_BUS_COV` `O`) is small enough for one request: `M.U2.B......EUR.O` lists 253 series in 1.4 s (checked 2026-11-24; the whole new-business slice is not), and it holds rates (`DATA_TYPE_MIR` `R`), the ECB's Bennet decomposition of each month's change (`I` the rate component, `J` the weight component, `M` and `N` their running totals since 2003-01, `O` the accumulated change, all in percentage points) and a coefficient of variation (`V`), and NO volumes (those are in `BSI`): list a slice and group its series by the data-type code and title before deciding which identity it can carry.
   When the queue's candidates have no exact relation to check (2026-11-25: the Federal Reserve's H.6 money stock parts do not add up to M2 and two of them end in 2020; the producer price indexes have no sum at all), ask the question the other way round: which release of an agency a card already uses has parts that add up? The building permits sit in the same Census release as the housing starts card, and all 8 ids answered at once with two exact sums. When no release a card already uses has such parts either, probe the ids of several other releases at once before taking the least bad queued source (one script, six threads: on 2026-11-25 the 45 ids of four releases answered in seconds and found new home sales, with an exact regional sum in all 141 months, a published ratio and an order check), and write the ids that answered and those that answered 404 in the queue note.
2. **Check it live from this machine** with a plain request (use `ATLAS_SEC_CONTACT` style polite User-Agent; never put the user's account email in a request). Record the HTTP status and a real sample. Time it COLD too (the ECB caches what it has just computed: repeat the request with an unusual `lastNObservations` such as 122), because a timeout fixed from warm answers is too short (MIR: 10 to 84 s cold, 0.1 to 0.9 s warm). If it cannot be reached here, mark it BLOCKED (note the host) and move to the next candidate. Never ship a source that was not fetched live in this cycle. FRED (`fred.stlouisfed.org/graph/fredgraph.csv`): ask for ONE series per request with `id` and `cosd` and no custom User-Agent; with several ids and one `cosd` it applies the date to the first series only and gives the others in full, without an error (JOLTS, 2026-11-18). Quarterly national accounts: consumer spending is `PCEC` (`PCE` is the monthly series and `DPCERC1Q027SBEA` answers HTTP 404; GDP, 2026-11-18); read each id's first dates before choosing it. Consumer spending by type is `PCDG`, `PCND`, `PCESV` (quarterly; the account codes on the series pages, such as `DDURRC`, are not FRED ids and the ids made from them answer HTTP 404), and household income, taxes, outlays and saving are the monthly `PI`, `W055RC1`, `A068RC1`, `PMSAVE` (2026-11-19). JOLTS by industry: FRED's ids are the Bureau's industry code with its trailing zeros cut to four digits (`JTS1000JOL` for total private, `JTS3000JOL` manufacturing) and six digits when the code ends in 99 (`JTS540099JOL`); only 16 industry series answer (mining and logging, information, financial activities, other services, wholesale trade, transportation, private education and the federal government answer HTTP 404 under every form tried), so probe the ids and count which parts of an identity exist before promising it (2026-11-19).
3. Check terms: public/open data or an API that allows this use, no login, no key, no scraping of pages that forbid it, no paid plan. Otherwise REJECTED with the reason.
   Read the notes of EVERY series you will use on its source page, not only the licence line (found 2026-11-17: a series that is calculated by a third party from the others, with its own copyright notice, sat among the three "parts" of a candidate and made its exact built-in check true by construction). A part that is derived from the others cannot check them, and a series with a third-party copyright notice is HELD, not shipped.
4. Build it as a provider with its own cache, timeout and a clear "unavailable" state (word the reason with `fail_reason(e)` from `atlas_api/failreason.py`, which also writes the ISO dates of a failed check in words, never with the raw exception: a test fails if a provider prints `{type(e).__name__}: {str(e)}`). Every figure carries source name, observation date and freshness. Never fill a gap with a guess. Save a small real sample as a test fixture.
5. Show it: a card on the relevant Signal tab (or an extra row in an existing card), unless it is a research-only input for the experiments (then record it under RESEARCH SOURCES in `atlas/DATA_SOURCES.md` instead). Label what it is and what it is not. Wire its refresh job by appending the new name at the END of the macro refresh list in `atlas_api/sched/jobs.py`: the tests of older cards pin the names beside theirs (on 2026-11-24 a name put between two of them failed the older card's `test_the_card_is_refreshed_with_the_other_macro_sources`).
6. Tests: backend pytest (add tests for parsing, failure and staleness), mobile typecheck/tests; if UI changed, `npm run export:web` then `node e2e/signal.e2e.cjs`. All must pass.
   Before the first breakage run, read the new test file against this list (each line was a survivor in a real table; loop-rules items 32, 35 and 36): (a) every fact the module has of its own, as a literal: the whole request address (path, host and every query parameter), the source string, each series id, each limit; (b) a module copied from an older one brings that file's class tests, so put the two lists of test names side by side; (c) each check in its order, with an input that fails it AND the next check; (d) every series' own end-month case, the total included, and the oldest month or week for every per-period check; (e) one assertion per fact a note quotes, starting at a delimiter so that the note's negation cannot satisfy it ("not seasonally adjusted" contains "seasonally adjusted"); (f) each rounded or ranked figure in a world where the right rule and its neighbours differ; (g) a tolerance that is not a binary number (0.1) cannot be hit at its edge, so its `>=` change is equivalent: list it as such with the reason, and keep the tolerances that ARE binary (2.5) pinned at the edge; (h) the longest failure message of each check, built from the constants with the widest figure it can carry, fits the 110 characters `failreason.py` keeps (found 2026-11-25 while writing the jobs by industry tests: the first wording of two sum messages ran to 116 and 117 characters and the cut would have dropped the tolerance at the end), so the test computes the lengths and does not list them. The run itself is `devtools/breakage.py` (README there): write only the table; its refusals name the pattern that is not found exactly once.
7. Update `atlas/DATA_SOURCES.md` and `atlas/IMPROVEMENT_LOG.md`, commit, push to `feature/research-modules`. Do not merge, keep the PR draft.
8. Reply with a 3-line update: **source added**, what it shows, where to see it (or why nothing shipped this cycle).

## Hard rules
- Free and keyless only. A key, a paid plan or a terms problem is a question for the user, not something to work around.
- No secrets in the repo; the SEC contact stays in the environment variable.
- No fabricated data, no demo data presented as real. Demo mode stays labelled.
- One source per cycle. If a cycle ships nothing, say so plainly.
- If tests fail and the cause is not obvious in one try, revert the change and log it.
- If the same blocker (for example a host this sandbox cannot reach) stops two cycles in a row, stop the loop's work and tell the user what is needed instead of repeating.
