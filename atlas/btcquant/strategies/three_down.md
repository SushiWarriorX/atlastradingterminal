# Reversal after a losing streak (`three_down`)

Protocol family 14, the only family pre-registered under protocol 1.4 · category: mean reversion · account kind: stop (the protocol's fixed 3 x ATR stop and 1% risk sizing, like the other two mean-reversion families) · written 2026-11-17, before any run of this family. No result of it has been looked at: the returns that followed runs of lower closes in the real candles have not been computed (the novelty screen in `RESEARCH_QUEUE.md` compared only WHEN the rule is invested with when the other families are, never what it earned).

## Hypothesis
After a run of lower daily closes, Bitcoin rebounds more than it usually does: selling pressure overshoots and part of it reverses. Buying after the third lower close in a row and selling at the first close that is not lower would earn that rebound while being invested only about one day in ten.

## Why it might work
- Short-term reversal is one of the oldest findings in equities: Jegadeesh (1990) reports that the previous month's losing stocks tend to outperform in the next month. **Status: from memory; the paper was not read for this note.**
- For Bitcoin, the sources recorded in `RESEARCH_QUEUE.md` (summaries returned by a web search on 2026-11-15; none was read in full): De Nicola finds negative first-order autocorrelation of Bitcoin returns at one, two and four hours on Bitstamp, with larger moves reversing more; Corbet and Katsiampa (2018) find asymmetric mean reversion in Bitcoin returns across frequencies; Quantpedia's daily backtests of buying local minima are mixed (less profitable but less risky than buying maxima, no significant seasonality in a later revisit, drawdowns above 80%). Volumes, pages and exact titles are not confirmed (`SOURCES.md`).
- **Against, and thin:** the Bitcoin evidence is mostly intraday, and no source found tests this daily rule on data after 2019; a falling market tends to keep falling (2022 is in the sample); and the two mean-reversion families already tested (`bollinger_mr`, `rsi_oversold`) were rejected. A study at one-hour candles says little about a rule that looks at daily closes.

## Where it should work, and where it should fail
It can work only if the day after a run of lower closes pays more than other days, by enough to cover what trading it costs. A completed trade costs 0.30% of the amount traded (0.10% fee plus 0.05% slippage, each way), and a trade holds about two candles (one, plus one more for every further lower close; about 2.0 if lower closes were independent coin flips), so the rebound must average above about 0.15% a day held just to pay for itself, and 0.30% a day to survive the doubled costs of admission gate A5. It should fail in a falling market that keeps falling (the rule buys each leg down), wherever the effect is absent or time-varying, and wherever a few large rebound days carry the average.

## Rules (completed daily candles only; the decision is made at the close of candle i and the order executes at the open of candle i+1)
- A lower close is a close strictly below the close of the candle before it. An equal close is not lower, and the first candle has no close before it. The run at candle i is the number of lower closes in a row that end at candle i.
- The state at the close of candle i is ON when the run is at least `streak` (the planned default is 3). A purchase is then ordered for the open of candle i+1, so the first candle held is the one after the `streak`-th lower close. While the run goes on the state stays ON; the first close that is not lower ends the run, the state goes OFF at that close and the sale is ordered for the next open. Because a run that has reached `streak` can only end by a close that is not lower, "the run is at least `streak` long" is the whole state. Nothing but closes up to candle i is read: no volume, no high or low except through the stop, no calendar.
- Stop: the protocol's fixed protective stop, the signal close minus 3 x ATR(14), set at entry and never moved. It is a disaster limit and not part of the idea: in a one-to-three-day trade it is rarely touched. A candle before the ATR exists (the first 14) cannot enter, because the account turns a signal without a usable stop down; this affects only January 2019, long before the evaluation starts.
- Re-entry as for every stop-based family: armed when OFF, one entry per run.

## Position sizing
Stop-based, as the protocol's section 4 says for families with a stop: size = the smaller of (equity x 1% divided by the distance between the entry fill and the stop) and (equity x 100% divided by the entry fill, with the fee reserved). With a stop about 3 ATR (about 10%) below the signal close that is typically 10% of equity in Bitcoin while the trade lasts, so raw returns are small by construction; every report shows the same signals at full exposure as a labelled sensitivity and an exposure-matched hold beside them. **Decided before any run:** the queue entry did not say how the rule is sized. It is stop-based, like `bollinger_mr` and `rsi_oversold`, and not a whole-account weight rule like `monday`: it has a usable stop, and a whole account that is in Bitcoin after every losing streak trips the 15% circuit breaker repeatedly (the `monday` account tripped it four times in 2020-2022), which would make the test about the breaker and not about the rule.

## Parameters (fixed grid; no value outside it may be tried without a new version)
Grid: {"streak": [2, 3, 4]}
Configurations: 3

`streak` is the length of the run of lower closes that starts a trade, and is the only setting. **As planned in `RESEARCH_QUEUE.md`: the grid was not changed.** The three settings are nested (a run of four is also a run of three and of two), so their results are strongly related, and the chosen one is selected on TRAIN by the mean of its own Sharpe ratio and its neighbours', as for every family. N grows from 101 to 104.

## Costs and risk limits
Fee 0.10% and slippage 0.05% per side on the amount traded; the four cost settings of the sensitivity runs apply. Cash earns nothing. Capped at 100% of equity, no leverage, no shorting. Circuit breaker at 15% below the running peak (research accounts resume after a 30-day cool-off).

## Failure modes
- Idea: no reversal at the daily horizon (the published evidence is intraday); continuation in falling markets, which 2022 contains; a rebound smaller than the cost of the trade; a result carried by a few very large rebound days; the rule is a near copy of `rsi_oversold` or `bollinger_mr` (its states correlate +0.12 with the closest configuration, so it is not a renamed copy, but it may fail for the same reason).
- Implementation: an off-by-one between the lower close that completes the run and the candle that is held (a test follows a run of five lower closes through the account engine to the exact fill rows and prices); an equal close treated as lower, or the first candle counted (tests); look-ahead (none is possible: the rule reads closes up to candle i, and the future-perturbation test covers every configuration); the rule written wrongly in a way both of my implementations share (the family and the novelty screen define it separately in code, and a test checks that they agree on every day of a 700-day random walk for runs of one to five).

## Gates, as pre-registered for this family (protocol 1.4)
Gates A1 to A5 apply as written, with no change (the amendment v1.4 changes no gate). A4 asks that at least 60% of the neighbours of the chosen setting reach 70% of its TRAIN Sharpe ratio: with three settings the middle one has two neighbours (both must reach it) and either end has one. Measured on synthetic data, before the real run (`CALIBRATION.md`, fourteen families: the family alone through selection and the five gates, 1,000 datasets per world): on pure noise it is admitted in 0.8% of datasets (8 of 1,000 in each of two worlds); with a planted reversal premium of 0.3%, 0.6% or 1.0% on every candle that follows three or more lower closes it is admitted in 5.7%, 15.9% and 38.3%. Every gate can be passed. The gate that costs the most power is A3, the random-timing test on VALIDATION alone (two years: about 20 trades of a streak-4 rule and about 90 of a streak-2 rule): even at a premium of 1.0% a day it failed in 59% of datasets, while its p-value was at or below 0.05 in 5.9% and 5.2% of the noise datasets, as it should be. A4 was not the obstacle (it failed in 4% of datasets at 1.0%); A2 and A5, which are largely cost hurdles, failed in 18% and 2% there and in 84% to 86% and 61% to 74% on noise. The procedure chooses an end setting (2 or 4) in 93% to 99.6% of the planted datasets and the middle one (3) in 0.4% to 6.7%, probably because the score of an end setting averages two Sharpe ratios and so varies more than the middle one's, which averages three; the default of 3 named in the queue is therefore rarely the setting that is tested on VALIDATION.

## Expectation written down before running
**Rejected.** The most likely reasons are gates A3 (timing), A2 and A5, which are largely cost hurdles for a rule that pays 0.30% for every run of lower closes: a rebound would have to be well above 0.15% a day held to show, and the procedure sees a premium of 1.0% a day about four times in ten, of 0.6% about 1.6 times in ten and of 0.3% about once in seventeen. **"Rejected" will therefore mean that no reversal premium large enough to see and to keep after costs was present in 2020 to 2024. It will not mean that there is no short-term reversal in Bitcoin.** "Too thin to judge" cannot happen (at least 32 trades on TRAIN plus VALIDATION in each of 5,000 synthetic datasets, and about 50 for the longest streak; A1 needs 10). If it were admitted, that would be unusual by luck on the synthetic data (about 1 in 125), but real prices have fat tails and volatility clusters that the synthetic data do not, so the real false-admission rate is unknown, and with fourteen families the chance that something among them is admitted by luck on a no-skill dataset is about one in six to one in five (`CALIBRATION.md`); admission would be a reason to watch the paper account, not evidence of an edge.
