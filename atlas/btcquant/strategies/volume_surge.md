# Volume surge continuation (`volume_surge`)

Protocol family 15, the only family pre-registered under protocol 1.5 · category: volume · account kind: stop (the protocol's fixed 3 x ATR stop and 1% risk sizing, like the other event-and-hold family `vol_breakout`) · written 2026-11-17, before any run of this family. No result of it has been looked at: the returns that followed heavy-volume up days in the real candles have not been computed. Two things were counted on the real candles to design it, and nothing else: how often volume surges (the shares of days at 1.5, 2 and 3 times the mean of the 20 days before it, to set the synthetic volume of `CALIBRATION.md`, from volume alone) and how many surge days there are in each period (149, 53 and 12 on TRAIN plus VALIDATION for ratios 1.5, 2 and 3, from volume and the sign of the day, to check admission gate A1). The novelty screen in `RESEARCH_QUEUE.md` compared only WHEN the rule is invested with when the other families are, never what it earned.

## Hypothesis
A day on which Bitcoin rises on unusually heavy volume is information that has not yet been fully priced, so the price keeps rising for a few more days. Buying at the open after such a day and holding for a few days would earn that continuation while being invested only about one day in eight.

## Why it might work
- The one academic source recorded in `RESEARCH_QUEUE.md`: Balcilar, Bouri, Gupta and Roubaud (2017) find with a nonparametric causality-in-quantiles test on daily data from December 2011 to April 2016 that trading volume predicted Bitcoin returns across most of the distribution (quantiles 0.25 to 0.75), except in bear and bull regimes, and did not predict volatility. **Status: the abstract, as recorded in the queue; the paper was not read in full for this note.** It tests predictability, not a trading rule, and it says nothing about the direction of the prediction; **this rule is our own proxy for it** and a failure of the rule would not contradict the paper.
- The general idea that unusual volume accompanies the arrival of information and that prices adjust to it over days is old and widespread (practitioner folklore: "volume confirms the move"). No rigorous test of this daily rule on Bitcoin was found.
- **Against, and thin:** volume spikes mark turning points in both directions (capitulation lows and blow-off highs) as often as the start of a run; the volume used here is one venue's (Coinbase BTC-USD), a small part of the market; the paper's own result excludes the bull and bear regimes in which most of Bitcoin's return is made; and the rule overlaps `vol_breakout` (the closest existing configuration, correlation +0.44 on the days it is invested), which was rejected.

## Where it should work, and where it should fail
It can work only if the days after a heavy-volume up day pay more than other days, by enough to cover what trading it costs. A completed trade costs 0.30% of the amount traded (0.10% fee plus 0.05% slippage, each way) and holds `hold` candles (3, 5 or 10, more when a second surge day falls inside the hold), so the continuation must average above about 0.10% a day held (hold 3) or 0.03% (hold 10) just to pay for itself, and double that to survive the doubled costs of admission gate A5. It should fail wherever a volume spike marks an end and not a beginning (2018-style capitulation, local tops), in a falling market that keeps falling, and wherever a few large days carry the average.

## Rules (completed daily candles only; the decision is made at the close of candle i and the order executes at the open of candle i+1)
- The BASELINE of candle i is the mean volume of the 20 candles before it, candles i-20 to i-1. The candle itself is not in its own baseline (so a volume of exactly twice the baseline of twenty equal days is a ratio of exactly 2.0, not 1.9). The first 20 candles have no baseline. A baseline that is not positive (no volume at all in the 20 days) is no baseline.
- A SURGE DAY is a candle whose volume is at least `ratio` times its baseline ("at least": equal counts) and whose close is strictly above the close of the candle before it (an equal close is not above; the first candle has no close before it).
- The state at the close of candle i is ON when a surge day fell on any of the last `hold` candles, candle i included. So a surge day at candle s turns the state ON at s, s+1, ..., s+hold-1: the purchase is ordered at the close of s for the open of s+1, the position is held through the `hold` candles s+1 to s+hold, and the sale is ordered at the close of s+hold (the first OFF close) for the open of s+hold+1. **A new surge day inside the hold starts the count again** (the position is then held `hold` candles after the newest surge day), so two surge days three candles apart with a hold of 3 make one trade of six candles, not two. This is how the novelty screen of `RESEARCH_QUEUE.md` already defined the rule when it was screened (`novelty.volume_surge`); the family and the screen are defined separately in code and a test checks that they agree on every day of a 900-day random walk for all nine settings.
- Stop: the protocol's fixed protective stop, the signal close minus 3 x ATR(14), set at entry and never moved. It is a disaster limit and not part of the idea: in a three-to-ten-day trade it is sometimes touched. A candle before the ATR exists (the first 14) cannot enter; the first possible surge day is candle 20, after the ATR exists.
- Re-entry as for every stop-based family: armed when OFF, one entry per ON period (a stop-out while the state is still ON is not followed by a new purchase until the state has gone OFF and ON again).

## Position sizing
Stop-based, as the protocol's section 4 says for families with a stop: size = the smaller of (equity x 1% divided by the distance between the entry fill and the stop) and (equity x 100% divided by the entry fill, with the fee reserved). With a stop about 3 ATR (about 10%) below the signal close that is typically 10% of equity in Bitcoin while the trade lasts, so raw returns are small by construction; every report shows the same signals at full exposure as a labelled sensitivity and an exposure-matched hold beside them. **Decided before any run:** the queue entry did not say how the rule is sized. It is stop-based, like `vol_breakout` (the other event-and-hold rule) and not a whole-account weight rule like `monday`, for the reason given for `three_down`: a whole account in Bitcoin after every surge would trip the 15% circuit breaker and the test would be about the breaker.

## Parameters (fixed grid; no value outside it may be tried without a new version)
Grid: {"ratio": [1.5, 2.0, 3.0], "hold": [3, 5, 10]}
Configurations: 9

`ratio` is how many times the baseline a day's volume must be, and `hold` how many candles the position is held after a surge day. The window of 20 days is fixed by the protocol and is not a setting. **As planned in `RESEARCH_QUEUE.md`: the grid was not changed**, although the real counts of surge days show that the ratio-3 row has only 12 surge days on TRAIN plus VALIDATION, which is close to the 10 trades that gate A1 asks for (see below). N grows from 104 to 113.

## Costs and risk limits
Fee 0.10% and slippage 0.05% per side on the amount traded; the four cost settings of the sensitivity runs apply. Cash earns nothing. Capped at 100% of equity, no leverage, no shorting. Circuit breaker at 15% below the running peak (research accounts resume after a 30-day cool-off).

## Failure modes
- Idea: volume spikes mark turnings as often as beginnings; one exchange's volume is a thin proxy for the market's; the effect, if any, is in regimes the source excludes; the rule is a near cousin of `vol_breakout` (correlation +0.44 on invested days: not a renamed copy, but it may fail for the same reason); a result carried by a few very large days; and the ratio-3 row may simply make too few trades to judge.
- Implementation: a baseline that includes the surge day itself (a test: 200 against twenty days of 100 is exactly 2.0, not 1.9); an equal ratio or an equal close treated the wrong way (tests at both boundaries); a zero baseline that lets any volume pass (a test); the hold counted from the wrong candle, or a second surge day inside a hold ignored (tests follow one surge and two surges through the account engine to the exact fill rows and prices); look-ahead (none is possible: the rule reads volume and closes up to candle i, and the future-perturbation test covers every configuration with a volume that changes from day to day); the rule written wrongly in a way both of my implementations share (the family and the novelty screen define it separately and a test checks that they agree).

## Gates, as pre-registered for this family (protocol 1.5)
Gates A1 to A5 apply as written, with no change (the amendment v1.5 changes no gate). A4 asks that at least 60% of the neighbours of the chosen setting reach 70% of its TRAIN Sharpe ratio: on this 3 x 3 grid the centre setting has four neighbours (three must reach it), an edge setting three (two must) and a corner two (both must). Measured on synthetic data, before the real run (`CALIBRATION.md`, fifteen families: the family alone through selection and the five gates, 1,000 datasets per world, with a synthetic volume that surges as often as the stored one): on pure noise it is admitted in 1.0% and 1.2% of datasets (10 and 12 of 1,000); with a planted premium of 0.10%, 0.20% or 0.40% on each of the five candles after a surge day (0.5%, 1.0% or 2.0% in all: 1.7, 3.3 and 6.7 times what a round trip costs) it is admitted in 1.5%, 2.8% and 6.2%. Every gate can be passed, but **the test has very little power for this family**: even a premium of 2.0% per surge day, 6.7 times the cost of the trade, was found in only 6.2% of datasets. The gate that costs the most is A3, the random-timing test on VALIDATION alone (two years: about 27 surge days at ratio 2.0 and fewer at ratio 3.0): it failed in 85% of datasets at the largest premium and in 91% to 95% elsewhere, while its p-value was at or below 0.05 in 5.2% and 5.7% of the noise datasets, as it should be. A4 failed in 44% to 74% of datasets and A2 in 41% to 69%; A5 in 2% to 21%. **A1 can fail**: in 6.5% to 13.3% of datasets the setting chosen on TRAIN was in the ratio-3 row, where the surge days are so few that fewer than 10 trades were made (the least was 2), and the outcome is then "too thin to judge". The procedure chooses ratio 1.5 in 54% to 69% of datasets and hold 10 in 50% to 66%, and the default (ratio 2.0, hold 5) in only 2% to 7%.

## Expectation written down before running
**Rejected, or too thin to judge.** The most likely outcome is a rejection on A3 (timing), A2 and A4; "too thin to judge" happens if the setting chosen on TRAIN is in the ratio-3 row (the real surge days there number 12 on TRAIN plus VALIDATION, against the 10 trades that A1 asks for; on synthetic data it happened in 6.5% to 13.3% of datasets). **A rejection will carry almost no information:** the procedure finds even a premium of 2.0% per surge day (6.7 times what the trade costs) in only 6.2% of synthetic datasets, so a rejection will mean that no premium larger than that was present in 2020 to 2024, not that volume does not predict Bitcoin's returns, and the paper's own result excludes the regimes where most of the return is made. If it were admitted, that would be unusual by luck on the synthetic data (about 1 in 100), but real prices have fat tails and volatility clusters that the synthetic data do not, so the real false-admission rate is unknown, and with fifteen families the chance that something among them is admitted by luck on a no-skill dataset is about one in six to one in four and a half (`CALIBRATION.md`); an admission would be a reason to watch the paper account, not evidence of an edge.
